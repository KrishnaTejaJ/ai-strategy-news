## 1. How America’s Dizzying AI Strategy Plays Into China’s Hands
**Source:** Center for Security and Emerging Technology  
**Link:** [How America’s Dizzying AI Strategy Plays Into China’s Hands](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-americas-dizzying-ai-strategy-plays-into-chinas-hands/)  

**So What:** The Trump administration's AI strategy may inadvertently bolster China's AI competitiveness, threatening US dominance.

The US administration's approach to promoting AI and semiconductor exports while addressing national security concerns is fragmented and potentially counterproductive, allowing China to capitalize on these inconsistencies. This dichotomy may undermine US efforts to maintain a technological edge over China. The situation highlights the need for a more cohesive and strategic approach to AI policy.

**Watch For:** Future developments in the US-China AI rivalry, particularly how China leverages perceived US strategic weaknesses to advance its own AI agenda.

## 2. Expanded Collaboration to Boost Critical AI Governance Data and Tracking Tool
**Source:** Center for Security and Emerging Technology  
**Link:** [Expanded Collaboration to Boost Critical AI Governance Data and Tracking Tool](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/expanded-collaboration-to-boost-critical-ai-governance-data-and-tracking-tool/)  

**So What:** Enhanced AI governance data and tracking capabilities can inform more effective policies and regulations in the AI domain.

The expansion of AGORA, a comprehensive collection of AI-related laws, regulations, and standards, will provide valuable insights into the evolving global AI governance landscape. This collaboration can facilitate more informed decision-making and help identify best practices in AI governance. The initiative may also encourage other countries to adopt more transparent and standardized approaches to AI regulation.

**Watch For:** The impact of AGORA's expanded capabilities on international AI governance discussions and the development of more cohesive global AI policies.

## 3. The True Cost of Cheap Chips
**Source:** War on the Rocks  
**Link:** [The True Cost of Cheap Chips](https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-true-cost-of-cheap-chips/)  

**So What:** The scarcity of critical semiconductor components, such as DRAM, may force major US tech companies to rely on suppliers with potential ties to the Chinese military.

The surging demand for computing power and the resulting shortages of key components have created a complex supply chain dilemma for companies like Apple, Dell, and HP. Their consideration of Chinese suppliers, despite national security concerns, underscores the challenges of decoupling from China in the tech sector. This situation may lead to increased scrutiny of supply chain risks and more stringent regulations.

**Watch For:** Potential US government responses to the supply chain risks posed by Chinese companies, including CXMT, and the implications for the global semiconductor industry.

## 4. How Indonesia Can Get Ahead in the Artificial Intelligence Race
**Source:** The Diplomat  
**Link:** [How Indonesia Can Get Ahead in the Artificial Intelligence Race](https://thediplomat.com/2026/08/how-indonesia-can-get-ahead-in-the-artificial-intelligence-race/)  

**So What:** Indonesia's abundant resources could support the development of a robust AI ecosystem, potentially altering the regional AI landscape.

Indonesia's unique combination of land, energy, and natural resources creates an opportunity for the country to establish itself as a key player in the AI sector, particularly in the development of hyperscale data centers. Successful leverage of these resources could enable Indonesia to attract significant investment and talent, possibly challenging the dominance of existing AI hubs. However, this would require strategic planning and investment in supporting infrastructure and expertise.

**Watch For:** Indonesian government initiatives and investments aimed at developing its AI sector, including potential partnerships with international companies and research institutions.

## 5. Can South Korea’s $880 Billion AI Ambition Help Cement Its Status as a High-Tech Powerhouse?
**Source:** The Diplomat  
**Link:** [Can South Korea’s $880 Billion AI Ambition Help Cement Its Status as a High-Tech Powerhouse?](https://thediplomat.com/2026/08/can-south-koreas-880-billion-ai-ambition-help-cement-its-status-as-a-high-tech-powerhouse/)  

**So What:** South Korea's significant investment in AI could solidify its position as a leader in the technology sector, particularly in chipmaking and related AI applications.

South Korea's ambitious AI plans, backed by substantial funding, are designed to capitalize on its existing strengths in semiconductor manufacturing and extend its leadership into AI. The success of this strategy depends on effective execution, including the development of necessary talent, infrastructure, and research collaborations. If successful, South Korea could emerge as a critical player in the global AI landscape, influencing both regional and international AI development and policy discussions.

**Watch For:** Progress in South Korea's AI initiatives, including the development of its chipmaking industry and the integration of AI technologies into its manufacturing sector.

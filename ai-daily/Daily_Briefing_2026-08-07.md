## 3. Trump meets AI giants, Senate Dems decry ‘unpredictable’ governance—and cheap Chinese AI looms as giant security risk
**Source:** Center for Security and Emerging Technology  
**Link:** [Trump meets AI giants, Senate Dems decry ‘unpredictable’ governance—and cheap Chinese AI looms as giant security risk](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/trump-meets-ai-giants-senate-dems-decry-unpredictable-governance-and-cheap-chinese-ai-looms-as-giant-security-risk/)  

**So What:** This matters because uncertainty in US AI governance, combined with cheaper Chinese AI, poses a risk to US competitiveness and national security.

The interplay between US AI governance uncertainty and the attractiveness of Chinese AI solutions due to cost and flexibility can significantly influence business decisions, potentially undermining US competitiveness. The situation highlights the need for clear, stable AI governance in the US to mitigate these risks.

## 5. A fundamental flaw leaves LLMs strikingly vulnerable to attack
**Source:** Artificial intelligence – MIT Technology Review  
**Link:** [A fundamental flaw leaves LLMs strikingly vulnerable to attack](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/30/1140927/a-fundamental-flaw-leaves-llms-vulnerable-to-attack/)  

**So What:** This matters because the inherent vulnerability of large language models (LLMs) to attacks has profound implications for AI security and governance.

The discovery of a fundamental flaw in LLMs that makes them impossible to secure fully against hacks is a critical finding with significant implications for the safety and reliability of AI technologies. This vulnerability could be exploited to manipulate or compromise AI systems, posing risks to data privacy, security, and the integrity of AI applications. It also highlights the need for a paradigm shift in AI development, focusing on security and robustness from the outset.

**Watch For:** Efforts to develop more secure LLMs and regulatory responses to mitigate the risks associated with their vulnerabilities.

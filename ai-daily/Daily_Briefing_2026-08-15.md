## 1. How America’s Dizzying AI Strategy Plays Into China’s Hands
**Source:** Center for Security and Emerging Technology  
**Link:** [How America’s Dizzying AI Strategy Plays Into China’s Hands](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-americas-dizzying-ai-strategy-plays-into-chinas-hands/)  

**So What:** The US AI strategy's inconsistencies may undermine its competitive edge over China.

The Trump administration's AI strategy is marked by contradictions, aiming to promote AI and semiconductor exports while addressing national security concerns. This inconsistency could hinder the US's ability to effectively compete with China in the AI sphere. Moreover, China may capitalize on these inconsistencies to advance its own AI agenda, potentially exacerbating the US-China tech rivalry.

**Watch For:** Future policy adjustments and their impact on the US-China AI competitiveness landscape

**Source:** Center for Security and Emerging Technology

## 4. The True Cost of Cheap Chips
**Source:** War on the Rocks  
**Link:** [The True Cost of Cheap Chips](https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-true-cost-of-cheap-chips/)  

**So What:** The global semiconductor supply chain's reliance on Chinese companies poses significant geopolitical risks.

The scarcity of electronic components, particularly DRAM, has led major companies like Apple to consider sourcing from Chinese firms, despite potential national security concerns. This underscores the complex interplay between economic pressures and geopolitical risks in the tech sector. As the US-China rivalry intensifies, the semiconductor supply chain's vulnerabilities may become increasingly exploited, leading to heightened tensions and potential disruptions.

**Watch For:** Future developments in the semiconductor supply chain, particularly regarding the role of Chinese companies and potential US policy responses

## 5. Indonesia has all that it takes to move up the value chain
**Source:** Atlantic Council  
**Link:** [Indonesia has all that it takes to move up the value chain](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/indonesia-has-all-that-it-takes-to-move-up-the-value-chain/)  

**So What:** Indonesia's economic growth and strategic position could make it a significant player in the global tech industry.

Indonesia's natural resources, young population, and strategic location provide a solid foundation for advancing its economic development and potentially moving up the value chain in the tech sector. As the country seeks to leverage these advantages, it may become an important partner for both the US and China in their ongoing tech competition. Indonesia's growth could also have implications for the global semiconductor supply chain, particularly if it chooses to invest in domestic chip production.

**Watch For:** Indonesia's economic development trajectory and its potential to become a key player in the global tech industry, particularly in the semiconductor sector

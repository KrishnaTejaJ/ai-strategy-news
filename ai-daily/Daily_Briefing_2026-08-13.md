## 1. How America’s Dizzying AI Strategy Plays Into China’s Hands
**Source:** Center for Security and Emerging Technology  
**Link:** [How America’s Dizzying AI Strategy Plays Into China’s Hands](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-americas-dizzying-ai-strategy-plays-into-chinas-hands/)  

**So What:** US AI strategy inconsistencies may compromise its competitive edge against China in the tech arena.

The Trump administration's AI strategy, while aiming to promote AI and semiconductor exports, may inadvertently create national security vulnerabilities that China could exploit. This could lead to a loss of US competitiveness in the global AI race. Furthermore, inconsistent policy approaches may undermine the development of a cohesive US AI governance framework.

**Watch For:** Future US policy announcements on AI and semiconductor exports to assess their potential impact on the US-China tech competition.

## 3. The True Cost of Cheap Chips
**Source:** War on the Rocks  
**Link:** [The True Cost of Cheap Chips](https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-true-cost-of-cheap-chips/)  

**So What:** DRAM supply chain vulnerabilities may force US companies to compromise on national security concerns, such as dealing with Chinese military-affiliated suppliers.

The surge in DRAM prices and supply shortages has led US companies like Apple to consider sourcing from Chinese companies with potential military ties. This dilemma highlights the need for more resilient and diversified semiconductor supply chains, particularly in critical components like memory chips. It also underscores the challenges of decoupling from Chinese suppliers in the tech sector.

**Watch For:** US government responses to Apple's request for assurances on CXMT, and potential policy initiatives to mitigate semiconductor supply chain risks.

## 4. Europe’s rare earth strategy is betting on allies
**Source:** Atlantic Council  
**Link:** [Europe’s rare earth strategy is betting on allies](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/europes-rare-earth-strategy-is-betting-on-allies/)  

**So What:** Europe's rare earth dependence on China necessitates deeper cooperation with allies to build more resilient supply chains.

Europe's inability to mine its way out of rare earth dependence on China has led to a shift in strategy, focusing on allied cooperation and investment in refining capabilities. This approach recognizes the limitations of unilateral action in addressing supply chain vulnerabilities. It may also create opportunities for EU-US collaboration on rare earth supply chain security.

**Watch For:** Concrete EU initiatives and investments in rare earth refining and recycling, as well as potential partnerships with non-Chinese suppliers.
